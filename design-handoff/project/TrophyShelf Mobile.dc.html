1TrophyShelf, rethought as a mobile app

The web build dresses finished books as sports trophies — dark walnut, brass plates, gold confetti. On a phone that reads as chrome rather than content: the covers, which are the only thing a reader actually wants to look at, end up framed in gold and shrunk to thumbnails. This rethink keeps the idea (a finished book is an achievement worth enshrining) but changes the metaphor from trophy cabinet to private library — the Classical system's tipped-in plate, hairline rules and gold-as-stroke. Covers get the space; gold marks the moment, never the whole surface.

Three shelf metaphors below, plus the full capture flow, the plaque moment, onboarding, the reading year, and one Android adaptation. Assumptions, since you want this production-bound: accounts and cloud sync exist (you unlocked them), so the shelf survives a lost phone; a currently-reading row sits above the shelf; every trophy can be shared as a card. Storage moves off localStorage to a real backend — 1i notes the stack I'd pick.
